The origin

Nala & Nila.
The builders.

From the Ramayana

Nala was the son of Vishwakarma, the divine architect. Nila was blessed so that anything he touched would float. Together, they led the construction of Rama Setu — a bridge across the sea that no one had built before, and most believed impossible.

I grew up hearing the Ramayana. Like most of us, I knew the broad strokes — the heroism, the exile, the war. But one part of the story always quietly stayed with me: Nala and Nila, the two brothers who built the bridge.

They didn't lead armies. They didn't wield weapons. What they brought was something rarer — craft and blessing working in tandem. Nala had the knowledge of an architect; Nila had a gift that made the impossible float. Neither alone could have done it. Together, they changed the course of everything.

When I started thinking seriously about what kind of company I wanted to build, I kept returning to that image. I had watched businesses — good ones, with good people — struggle not because they lacked ambition, but because they lacked the right tools. They were spending enormous energy on things that could be handled: repetitive decisions, slow workflows, information that sat in silos, processes that should have run themselves.

What if technology could be what Nila's gift was — the thing that makes the heavy things float, so the real builders can focus on building?

That's the idea at the heart of Nalanil Labs. We combine the structured thinking of the architect with the practical power of AI, automation, and technology — not to replace people, but to lift the weight off them. To give small and mid-sized businesses the kind of capability that was once only available to the very large.

The name Nalanil carries both brothers — inseparable, complementary. That's intentional. We don't believe in technology for its own sake, and we don't believe in strategy without the means to execute it. The bridge only got built because Nala knew how, and Nila made it possible.

We're here to do the same for the businesses we work with. One bridge at a time.
